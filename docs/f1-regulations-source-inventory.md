# FIA 2026 Formula One Regulations: Source Inventory

Research date: **2026-09-10**. Scope: [Racing Hub issue #11][issue], official FIA 2026 regulations and a narrow race-points question. This is primary-source research and a candidate evidence inventory, not regulation ingestion, a published Competition Profile, legal clearance, or human approval.

## Finding

**Research paraphrase:** Under the fetched 2026 rules, a sole first-place finisher in the final race classification receives **25 race points** in the full-points band. That band applies when the leader completes at least 75% of the Scheduled Race Distance, subject in every case to the leader completing at least two complete, consecutive laps without a Safety Car or Virtual Safety Car procedure. This supports a conditional full-race winner example, not an assertion about any actual Grand Prix. The controlling allocation is **Section A, A2.2.1**, not Section B. [A, PDF pages 9-10, printed A9-A10][a-points]

No fastest-lap bonus is specified in the reviewed A2.2.1 allocation. Do not add a bonus from an older season, or treat this scoped finding as an exhaustive search of every FIA instrument. Sprint points are a separate provision, A2.2.2, and are outside the proposed race example. [A, PDF page 10, printed A10][a-table]

### Conditional Answer And Refusal Boundary

**Candidate answer, paraphrased, not approved for publication:** "For a sole winner in a 2026 Formula One race that qualifies for full points, the allocation is 25 points. Section A Issue 03, A2.2.1(d) and the first-place entry in column 4 on PDF page 10 (A10) establish the amount. The opening paragraph on PDF page 9 (A9) requires two complete, consecutive leader laps without SC/VSC procedures, and the full-points band requires at least 75% of scheduled distance. This assumes final first-place classification, no dead heat, and no applicable decision changing the result or award." [A9][a-points] [A10][a-table] [A11][a-tie] [B25][b-classification]

## Method, Rights And Blockers

The official index HTML was parsed with a DOM parser to discover exact A/B links, including B's `_7.pdf` suffix. Ordinary public HTTPS GET requests downloaded the index and PDFs to temporary storage outside the repository. PowerShell SHA-256 hashes cover the received files, not extracted text. PDF.js extracted native PDF text and rendered the two points pages for layout/colour checks. No OCR, secondary-source rule tables, 2025-rule substitution, credentials, access-control bypass, or agents were used. The research file is the only repository artifact; full PDFs, full extracted text, and page images are not repository fixtures.

**Source rights caveat:** Both PDFs carry FIA copyright notices. Public availability, the user's research authorization, a checksum, and a small quotation budget do not grant a licence for ingestion, embedding, translation, redistribution, training, or production use. The index links [FIA Terms and Conditions][terms], but those terms and any licence grant were not evaluated in this pass. Rights/permission review remains unresolved. Retain only this narrow factual inventory and minimal excerpts; broader evidence persistence needs an appropriate rights determination and explicit review.

**Blockers:** There was no FIA retrieval blocker for the index or either current PDF. Research supports the conditional winner example, but does not complete Issue 11's ingestion, Competition Profile, translation/review workflow, or assistant implementation. Human evidence approval, a rights determination, historical/event-specific applicability checks, and unreviewed external documents remain gates for those uses. A later fetch with a different hash must be treated as new candidate evidence, not silently substituted under an existing approval.
